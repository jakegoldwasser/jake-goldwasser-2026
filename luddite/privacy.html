Luddite Privacy Policy

Last updated September 30, 2026

Luddite is a locked-down, full-screen writing room for classes, made by Jake Goldwasser, a teacher, at jake-goldwasser.com/luddite. This page explains what it keeps and why. It's short because Luddite keeps very little.

Signing in

You sign in with Google. Luddite receives your name, email address, and Google's ID for your account, and uses them only to know who you are: so your teacher can let you into a room, and so your handed-in work has your name on it. Luddite never sees your Google password.

What students' work and activity Luddite keeps

  • Rooms you join: your name and email in that room's list, whether you're waiting, let in or removed, and when you asked to join.
  • While you write: your word count and how many times you left the full-screen page, so your teacher can see that you're writing. The text you're writing stays in your own browser until you hand it in.
  • What you hand in: the text, word count, and time. It's kept so your teacher can read it, comment on it, and print it, and it's shown only to that teacher (and to Luddite's owner, who runs the service).
  • Your teacher's comments on work you've handed in.

Teachers and Google Drive

A teacher can connect their Google Drive so that each handed-in piece is also saved as a Google Doc in a "Luddite submissions" folder in their Drive. Luddite asks only for Google's drive.file permission, which lets it create and see the files Luddite itself makes, and nothing else in the teacher's Drive. Luddite stores a token that lets it save those files while the teacher is away; the teacher can remove it any time from their Google Account's security settings, or by connecting a different account in Luddite.

What Luddite doesn't do

  • No ads, no tracking scripts, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone other than your own teacher.
  • Luddite's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.
  • Nothing you write is used to train AI models.

Where it's stored

Luddite's data is stored in Amazon Web Services (DynamoDB, in the United States), sent over encrypted connections. Copies saved to a teacher's Drive are in that teacher's Google account.

Deleting your information

A teacher can delete a room, which removes it from their list. To have your account's information or handed-in work deleted from Luddite entirely, email jake.goldwasser@gmail.com and it will be removed within 30 days. Files already saved in a teacher's Drive belong to that teacher.

Students

Luddite is meant to be used in class, set up by a teacher. Schools that use it are responsible for any consent their students' ages or local rules require.

Changes and contact
