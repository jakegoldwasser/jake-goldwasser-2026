## Luddite (`luddite/`)

Use these words, and only these, in the UI, in conversation and in commit messages (one word per idea, so a teacher always knows what a button means):

- **Writing session** — what a teacher opens for a class (never "room" or "assignment"; the code and the stored data still say `room`, and the API routes say `assignment`, which is left alone). Named for the work, never an auto date/time name. A session is **Happening now**, **Coming up** or **Past**, shown in those three groups on Home, and has a start time; it runs until the teacher ends it (the date and time are a stamp). Every session has an **Entry Phrase**; there are no permanent or standing rooms.
- **Casual session** — a writing session with no name, no class and no waiting room: one button on Home starts it now, and anyone who types its Entry Phrase is let straight in (`casual: true`). It is named by its prompt's first line once it has a prompt (e.g. "Write five favorite breakfast cereals", cut at about 60 characters), otherwise by its Entry Phrase (e.g. "Copper valley"), never "Casual session"; a name the teacher gives it (Rename, `titled: true`) wins over both; `titleOf` in the backend also shows the phrase for the first casual sessions, which were stored as "Casual session".
- **Class** — a list of email addresses a teacher makes by pasting or uploading (.csv/.txt). A session can be given a class: when it starts, everyone on the list who signs in is let in on their own (no waiting room). Anyone else can still type the Entry Phrase and wait in the waiting room.
- **Alternative schedule** (once "own time"; the code and stored data still say `own`) — one student's own schedule inside a session: a different start, extra time (1.5×, 2×, or other), or both. The button is **Set Alternative Schedule**. These students are listed in their own **Alternative schedule** panel just below Students (`secOwn`), and **End session** doesn't end theirs: it closes ten minutes after their time ends (`OWN_GRACE_MS`, page and backend; not late until after that), when they hand in, or when the teacher (⋯ › Hand in for them now) or any proctor (**Hand in now**) closes it (`handIn` action, `handInFromBackups` in the backend). An alternative schedule can be **In parts** (`own.blocks`: [{ start, end }], `cleanBlocks`, `partsWindow`; in the dialog each part is a period or other time and Whole period / First half / Second half / Minutes, `tbAddPart`, `tbBlocks`): a student takes the paper in pieces, in any periods (say the first half of D, then all of H), paused between parts like days (the student's page says Part, not Day), handed in ten minutes after the last. Parts in back-to-back periods (only a passing period between) are saved as one continuous part, so the student writes straight through the passing period (`joinAdjacent`; `splitAdjacent` shows them as periods again in the dialog); the 20-minute break isn't a passing period. A proctor's **Begin the next part now** moves the next part to now.
- **Unlocking always lets a student write** (Jake, 2026-10-20): a student unlocked or let write again (`unlockedAt`/`releasedAt`) after the session ended or their own time ran out is reopened (`reopenedAt` in `windowOf`): no time limit, until they hand in, someone hands in for them, or the teacher ends the session again. A proctor's Unlock also unlocks a handed-in student. The time picker follows the Horace Mann day: periods A–H in order, 45 minutes with 5 between, 8:25 to 3:15, break 10:00–10:20 (`DAY`, `PERIODS` in the page; the real day rotates the letters, so "Other time" covers that).
- **Hide names** — one button top right on every teacher page (`#topRight`, beside Settings), that swaps every student's name for a stable alias ("Student K7Q") everywhere: Home, the session page, grading, a student's page, print. While names are hidden, **Names key** beside it opens a list of the aliases on the page showing and the names behind them (`anonSeen`, filled by `who()`, reset by `show()`), with **Print** (a plain table in a window of its own). Drive copies still carry the real name, so "Open in Drive" is hidden while names are hidden.
- **Settings** — top right on teacher pages: what students get while writing (spellcheck, page lock, copy/paste, word count, auto hand-in), the owners' teacher list, and Account (display name, Google Drive, Write on my own, Sign out).
- **For you to write** — on Home, the sessions a teacher is in as a writer (another teacher gave one to a class they are on).
- **Proctor** — someone who runs a writing session without seeing any writing: they **let in** from the waiting room, **unlock** anyone locked out, **begin** a student's own time now, and **pause** / **resume** (a paused student can't write, can leave the page without being locked, and their time stops; `pausedAt`/`pausedMs` on the student entry or the room, applied in `windowOf`). A **permanent proctor** proctors every student with own time in every session, plus the waiting room: `testingcenterud@horacemann.org` (the Upper Division testing center, `TESTING_CENTER_EMAILS`) always, and anyone owners add under Settings › **Permanent proctors** (`luddite:proctors`; owners' own addresses are refused, so Jake can't reach HM sessions that way). A teacher can **Add a proctor…** to one session (`room.proctors`, More menu), who proctors everyone in it and can also start, pause or resume the whole session. Proctors land on **To proctor** (`proctorScreen`, `proctorRoomScreen`; its **Coming up** button, also on a teacher-proctor's Home panel, opens every future time they're needed by day: each alternative-schedule student, each part of one in parts, and whole sessions they were added to, with Print; `openComingUp`, built from `/luddite/proctor/session` views, so no writing; `/luddite/proctor*` in the backend, which sends no writing, drafts or messages). Sessions with own time are listed for permanent proctors from `luddite:proctoring`, filled in when own time is set.
- **At home** — "Let students work on this at home" (`room.atHome`; a checkbox in New writing session, and in a session's More menu): writing is still full screen, but leaving it never locks a student out (it still counts as leaving the page; they go back with one click), the writing page has **Leave for now** beside Hand in (also on the lock screen), and the session runs with no time limit until the teacher ends it. Switching it on lets back in anyone locked out. Closing the page sends the writing to the server (`pagehide`, keepalive), and a newer server copy replaces an older one on another computer (`restoreDraft`).
- **Classmates' work** — "Allow students to see each other's work" (`room.shareWork`, a session's More menu, on or off any time): while it's on, students let in get a **Classmates' work** button on the writing page that opens everyone else's writing so far, read-only (`GET /luddite/classwork`, names and text only, refreshed every 15 seconds while open).
- **Student rows** on a session's page (`personFacts`, `personRow`): a **padlock** at the left (open and green: can write; shut and red: can't, for any reason: handed in, locked out, paused, not started, session ended — Jake asked for this back on 2026-10-20, replacing the pencil; clicking it locks or unlocks after a warning, `lockToggle`: locking someone who can write hands in their paper now, `handIn`; unlocking is `release`/`unlock`, a paused student's is Resume), one plain line of where they are, at most one button only when the teacher is needed (Unlock, Let them back in, Resume, Share Grading), and on the right one **status bubble** (`STATUS`, `f.status`, `.statusbub`): **In progress** (gray), **Grade!** (pink), **Graded** (yellow), **Grading shared** (green; once shared it stays so, whatever is edited after — only Stop sharing on the marking page undoes it). A paper's bubble opens its grading (there's no separate Grade button or "Open the paper" item); comments and the grade can change any time. "Nothing handed in" (gray) when the session is over with no paper. No eye icon. **Unlock all** in the Students heading unlocks everyone locked out or handed in.
- **End session** (the running session's button; never "End it now") hands in everyone still writing from their backups and locks them out (the student's page hands in whatever the Settings say), except students on an alternative schedule.
- **Days** — a writing session's start, at a set time, is a schedule: one block per day, and in it one time per class picked (each at its class's period by default; changing one class's date moves the day's others), **+ Another day** to add one (`newDays`, `buildDays`). With several days, **The days** picks **A new paper each day** (a session per class per day) or **One paper over all the days** (the default; not for casual or at-home sessions): one session per class with `room.sittings` ([{ start, end }], a period's end, else 45 minutes). It runs only during its sittings: between them everyone is paused (`sittingsWindow` in the backend: `paused`, `sitting`, `sittings`, `sittingEnd`, `nextAt`), the student's clock counts down "left today" and stops writing at the day's end (`dayOver`), and auto hand-in waits for the last day. Extra time stretches every sitting; own start times don't apply. Home and the session's page list the days and say **Between days · next …**.
- **Class colors** — each class has a color (`CLASS_COLORS`, eight; `class.color`, picked on the Class page, else the next one unused, oldest class first: `classColor`), shown as a small **square with the class's period letter in it** (never a circle, which reads as a status mark; `classDot`, `.cdot`) wherever the class is named: Home's session cards, the class buttons and day rows in New writing session, the class list, a session page's title.
- **Period** — a class's period letter (A–H, `class.period`). Picking classes for a new writing session (toggle buttons, several allowed) starts it at the class's period by default; several classes make one session each, each at its own class's period on the day picked.
- **Prompt** — typed in a rich field (`richField`: line breaks, Cmd/Ctrl+B, I, U; stored with the writing's markers and shown with `fmtInto`), in New writing session and in Edit the prompt (`ask({ input: 'rich' })`, a pop-up that resizes from its corner and keeps its size). A session's page shows its class big above the title (`#roomClass`: the class square, **Period D · class name**).
- **Entry Phrase** — the two words students type to join (never "code", "room code" or "code word"; `code` is fine inside the code).
- **Home** — the teacher's first page (not "Dashboard"). **Student work** is its list of everyone who has handed work in; a student's page is everything they've handed in. A past session's page lists its papers first.
- **Waiting room** — where students who aren't on the class list ask in. The teacher **lets them in**; a teacher **removes** someone.
- **Hand in / Handed in** — what a student does with their writing in a writing session (never "submit"). "Done" is only for writing on your own. A handed-in student is **locked** until the teacher **unlocks** them ("Unlock so they can write again").
- **Paper** — one handed-in piece of writing (not "piece" or "submission"; the Drive folder is literally named "Luddite submissions", which is left alone). **Mark up** is the verb; teachers write a **comment** in the margin, **cross out** words, **write in** words, draw an **arrow** between two points in the text (right-drag, or Control-drag on a Mac; it gets a numbered comment in the margin about the connection, numbered together with the comments), add an **end comment** and a **grade**. On the marking page the text is split into `.seg` spans; never reuse that class name for anything else (the choice-button rows are `.segctl`). The teacher's pen is green; red means locked or a warning. **Print all papers** is a button on a session's page (every paper handed in to it) and on a student's page (everything they've handed in); each paper prints marked up, one to a page.

- **Rubric** — made under Settings › Rubrics, or from a session's More › Rubric… (+ New rubric…), in one of four ways: **Make a grid** (skills × levels, scored on the marking page), **Paste it in** (kept as plain paragraphs and tables, `blocks`, by `blocksFrom`), **Upload a PDF**, or **From Google Docs** (Google's Picker as the same account as Luddite, `drive.file`; the doc is exported to a PDF, `docId` kept for "Get the latest"). The last three are `kind: 'doc'`, there to read, not score; a PDF is stored apart in `luddite:rubricfile:<id>` (`/luddite/rubricfile`, up to 2.5 MB) and drawn with pdf.js. **Show students the rubric while they write** (`room.rubricShown`, a checkbox in the Rubric… dialog, `chooseRubric`; on by default, and always on for a new rubric) gives students a **Rubric** button beside Hand in. **Print rubrics** on the print page puts each student's rubric (their scored grid, else the session's) right after their paper. The Picker works without an API key (`PICKER_KEY` in the page is optional; checked 2026-10-19).
- **Classes are in the teacher's order** — oldest first, dragged by the grip on Home's class list (`classesOrdered`, `/luddite/classes/order`).
- **Student formatting:** students bold, italicize and underline with Cmd/Ctrl+B, I, U only (no buttons). The writing box (`#pad`) is a contenteditable with a textarea-like `value`; the stored text carries markers `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `__underline__` (per line, `\` escapes a literal `*`, `_` or `\`), read by `fmtScan`. Marks' offsets count the markers, so the marking page keeps them in hidden `.fmk` spans; use `plainOf` for anything quoted or counted. Drive copies still show the markers as typed characters.
- **Live writing**: no snippet of a student's writing in the Students list; clicking a student who can still write opens it whole (`liveBox`, from `GET /luddite/watch`; students' pages send it every `HEARTBEAT_MS`), with their live comments in its right margin (a wrapping box that grows to show the whole comment; Enter sends) and Previous / Next through the writers in the list's order. While the session runs, a **Live comments** widget sits bottom right (`liveDock`, `drawLiveDock`; foldable, and its × shrinks it to a speech-bubble icon with the unread count, `dockMin`; both remembered on the device): everyone with a thread, unread first, and the **Announcement** button (once "Message everyone"). A new live comment or raised hand marks the row and sorts it to the top. All of it hides while the server answers 404.
- **Live comments** (once "Messages"; never "chat" in the UI; the code, routes and stored data still say `chat`, and ids say `msg…`): while a student writes in a session, the teacher and that student can write to each other. The student's **Live comments** window sits in the right margin of the writing page (`#msgSide`; the writing moves over to make room, and below 1000px it lies over the writing); the student can hide it (remembered on the device, `luddite.msgsHidden`) to a tab at the right edge, which shows a green bubble (`#msgDot`) while a message from the teacher is unread. **Raise hand** (in the window and on the tab) opens a box for a question or comment and sends it as a message with `hand: true` (`handAt` on the student's entry); the student's row says **Hand raised** until it's read, and the message is labeled Raised hand. The teacher writes from the student's opened row (or **Announcement** in the Live comments widget). The teacher's messages are in the green pen. `GET`/`PUT /luddite/chat`, one item per pair (`luddite:chat:<code>:<sub>`); the room's student entry keeps `chatT`/`chatS` (last sent) and `seenT`/`seenS` (last read), which the heartbeat and the session page's poll already carry. The opened student steps through the writers with Previous / Next (or the arrow keys), in the Students list's order.

Grading steps through a session's or student's papers with Previous / Next, and **Share Grading** (once called Hand back; the code and stored data still say `handBack`, `returnedAt`) lets the student see the comments (and the grade after five minutes reading them), then opens the next paper not yet shared. ## Bookbug (`bookbug/`)

- The app's name is **Bookbug** — one capital B. Never "BookBug", "Book Bug", or "BookLouse". The lowercase `bookbug` wordmark in the page header is a logo and stays lowercase. This applies everywhere: UI text, `<title>`, comments, commit messages, docs.
- Use these terms consistently in the UI and in conversation: **book title**, **section title**, and each book type's own word for its parts — **poem** in a poetry collection, **chapter**/**part** in a novel, **essay**/**section** in essays, **scene**/**act** in a screenplay or play (never "heading", "chapbook", or "body text"; "entry" only in code and in text that covers every type at once). UI strings take these from `TEXT_FORMS` (`formNouns()`, `data-noun` spans filled by `applyFormWording`), never hard-coded. Internal identifiers such as `state.poems`, `chapbookId`, and the `chapbookbuilder` cloud key are legacy names; leave them alone rather than renaming stored data.
- A book is either on Home (the book grid, formerly called "My Books"), archived, or deleted. Delete is only offered from the archive, except for a text book's entries (poems, chapters, essays, scenes), whose ⋯ menu also has **Delete…**: it skips the archive, and Restore puts the entry back at its old place in the book (`from: 'book'`, `at`). A cartoon's ⋯ menu has the same **Delete…** beside Archive (cartoon batch `deleted`, `from: 'book'`, `at`). **Deleting is recoverable for 7 days** (`RECOVER_DAYS`; entries in `deletedPoems` for 30, `ENTRY_RECOVER_DAYS`): a deleted book goes to **Recently deleted** at the bottom of Home (`recentlyDeleted`, synced; the Lambda merges it by id and drops entries past 7 days or since restored), and within a book, deleted archived poems/chapters (`state.deletedPoems`), spreads deleted or emptied from the archive (picture book `deleted`) and emptied cartoons (cartoon book `deleted`) show in a Recently deleted part of that book's archive panel. Restore puts it back where it was deleted from (the archive, or a spread's old place). Confirmations for these say it can be restored, on a red Delete button.
- **Archive, don't delete.** Removing anything that has content in it (a book, poem/chapter/essay/scene, section/part/act, spread, picture-book text box or illustration, or a cleared spread's contents) sends it to its archive, where it can be restored. The only exception: something with no information at all (empty entry, untitled empty section, empty spread, empty text box, a book with nothing of the author's in it) is removed outright, skipping the archive. The untouched sample a new novel, essay collection, screenplay or play starts with doesn't count as content, and an import replaces it. A button labeled "Archive" only ever archives something. Each archive (a book's, a picture book's, a cartoon book's, Home's) is its own panel, shown only while it holds something, and opened or closed by the Show / Hide on that panel (`syncArchivePanel`); nothing elsewhere toggles it.
- Bookbug has eight **book types**: **poetry collections** (`form: 'collection'`; poems, each starting on a page of its own), **novels** (chapters and parts), **essay collections** (essays and sections), **screenplays** and **stage plays** (scenes and acts; see below), **picture books**, and **cartoon batches** (the UI says "cartoon batch", never "cartoon book"; code and stored data still say `cartoonBooks`, `cb…`; a drawing and a caption per page, in the editor each caption is typed with its drawing on one white sheet (a **Caption below / Caption beside** switch atop the list, `prefs.cbCaptionSide`, this device only; beside is larger type), the field showing it as set (curly quotes, dashes, `cbShownCaption`; no separate "Set as" line); quotation marks are a batch option in Formatting, **Captions in quotation marks** (`quoted`), never a per-cartoon checkbox: the marks live in the caption text (old `spoken` captions folded in by `cbNormalizeCartoon`), the option wraps or unwraps every caption and wraps new ones as typed; captions set New Yorker style in italic: Adobe Caslon Pro where installed, else EB Garamond (`CAPTION_STACK`; Libre Caslon Text looked too heavy and wide next to the magazine's) by default, and Formatting can change a batch's caption font (`CAPTION_FONTS`, faces the page already loads), size, bold and italic (`captionFont`, `captionSize`, `captionBold`, `captionItalic`; editor fields follow via `--cb-cap-*`); no cover or title page, since a cartoon book is a batch; `cbLibrary`, its own editor, synced as `cartoonBooks`; its import dialog takes a PDF, chosen photos, or photos taken live with the camera, `getUserMedia`, as many as wanted until Done; there is no loop/circle detection any more (Jake found it cut pages down to any closed shape in a drawing, and takes a close-up photo of each doodle instead; don't bring it back); a printed cartoon's heavy box line is whited out, `scanStripFrame`; The table round a photographed page is whited out only where it's big and solid (`scanOffMask`), so a pen line running off the edge of a close-up stays. Each imported cartoon keeps its cleaned page as an image (`images[id].page`, not preloaded) and where it was cut from (`crop`), so **Frame** on a cartoon (or ⋯ › Frame the drawing…) can move it, size it, turn it 90° or straighten it by any angle on the page, `cbOpenFrame`; the turn is `rot`, degrees clockwise, and `crop` is in fractions of the turned page's bounding box. In a novel a blank chapter title shows as "Chapter N" and a blank part "Part One" by position; in a play, "Scene N" (restarting each act) and "Act One". Novels and essays default to book-style prose paragraphs (`paragraphs: 'indent'`: a half-inch first-line indent, the same as a Tab, shown in the editor too via `tagProseLines`; a line of `***` is a scene break). Home shows a shelf only for each type the person has (unarchived) books of ("Poetry collections", "Novels", "Essay collections", "Picture books", each shelf color-coded in its editor colorway; every book on Home is the same square card, its title in Zilla Slab whatever the type, told apart only by its type's color (a spine stripe down the left edge) and a small line icon (`COVER_ICONS`, `homeCoverHTML`): crescent moon and stars for poetry, tome for novels, clipboard for essays, film camera for screenplays, comedy/tragedy masks for plays, rabbit in profile for picture books, inkwell for cartoon batches, speech bubble for comics; **New book** — one big blank white page at the top left of Home, Docs style, plus one entry at the top of the Home menu, never one per type — opens one dialog: a title, then which book type, with a picture book's pages/shape/front matter in the same dialog); to its right, **Recent** shows the most recently edited books of every type, newest first, one row of as many as fit (`renderHomeRecent`; hidden until there are two books), so someone making only one kind never sees the other. The shelves' order is the person's: drag a shelf by its grip (pointer events, so phones too; arrow keys on the focused grip), stored with `homeForms` and followed by the Home menu. A book's title shows only on its cover on Home, not repeated under it. Preferences are grouped: the text book types share one group, picture books have their own; every group for a type on Home is shown, the one for the book you're in first and open, the others folded under their labels (all open on Home). The text group's **Tab indent** (¼–1 in, default ½ in like Docs/Word; `prefs.tabIndentPt` → `TAB_STOP_PT`) sets both a Tab and a prose paragraph's first-line indent, in the editor and in print. Verse lines' hanging indent uses `text-indent: … hanging`, never padding plus a negative indent (that pulled every tab stop back).
- A picture book is laid out by the **spread**: page 1 alone, numbered spreads (a verso and a recto page), and the last page alone, so n spreads make 2n − 2 pages. Pages can have a **role** (title page, copyright, endpaper, pastedown…). Spreads hold free-placed **text boxes** and **illustrations** (the UI says "illustration", never "picture", for the art; code still says `image`/`pic`). Each spread's notes are **illustration notes**. A spread's **+ Illustration** opens the cartoon batch's importer (`pbOpenScan`, `openCartoonImport` with a `sink`; Scan as starts on Color): photos, a PDF or the live camera, cleaned up the same way, a page of several drawings cut apart; the first goes on that spread, the rest on the next spreads without an illustration, any past the end into the archive (`pbPlaceScanned`). A PNG, WebP or GIF there is finished digital art (`digitalArt`) and comes in untouched apart from Scan as and Trim. A file dropped on a spread still goes straight in. Text boxes can bold or italicize single words: the text holds the book editor's `**bold**` / `*italic*` markers, edited in rich fields (`pbRichField`, a contenteditable with a textarea-like `value`), set word by word everywhere it's drawn (`pbWrapRich`, `pbRunFont`) and as separate runs in PowerPoint; B / I (or Cmd/Ctrl+B, I) style the selected words, or the whole box with nothing selected. Title, author and byline boxes stay plain. A text box can set its words on a **path** (`path`: points in pt from the box's corner, `closed` for a shape): the quick bar's **Curve ▾** offers Arc, Wave, Circle, **Draw a line or shape…** (a stroke across the spread; nearly straight comes out a straight line, ending where it began closes it), Flip to the other side and Straight again; a spread's ⋯ menu has **Draw words on a line or shape…** for a new box. The words run on one line letter by letter, placed at the start, middle or end by the box's alignment, set smaller if they're longer than the path. One layout (`pbPathLayout`) draws the editor (SVG), the preview and PDFs (canvas, `pbDrawPathText`) and PowerPoint (baked into the slide's art, since PowerPoint can't set text on a path). A spread's ⋯ menu has **Delete…**, which skips the archive: immediately if the spread is empty, otherwise after a confirmation. A **Bring in story text** panel at the foot of the spreads (folded to a bar, like a poetry collection's Bring in panel; `pbBringInStanzas`) does the same in an open book: each stanza onto the next story spread without words, adding spreads two at a time after the story if needed, one Undo. The New book dialog's picture-book options take **Story text**, pasted or uploaded (.txt, .md or a PDF): each line starting with a number begins a stanza (the dashes, colons, periods or brackets right after the number are dropped; lines without a number continue the stanza above), and each stanza becomes the words of one story spread, on its left page (`pbParseStanzas`, `pbFillStanzas`); the book grows four pages at a time if there are more stanzas than story spreads (`pbNewBookFor`). Exports: digital dummy, printable dummy, PowerPoint. Story Plotter (`/storyplotter/`) isn't in the site nav any more; it's reached from the **Story Plotter** panel in a picture book's tools column. It is the old Dummy Builder folded into Bookbug; don't bring Dummy Builder back as a separate tool.
- A picture book has three views: **Text** (the default: each page's words in large fields), **Spread** (as printed; double-click a page to add a bottom-center text box; click a box to select it, click again to edit it in a pop-up that opens right over the box as a magnified copy of it (same line breaks; the caret goes where you clicked, a double-clicked word comes up selected; `pbPopPick`)) and **Thumbnail**. In Text view each text box has a grip to drag it to any page in the book (pointer events, so fingers work too; `pbMoveTextToPage`), and two boxes on the same page can be **merged** (Merge on the line between them, or in Spread view's quick bar; `pbMergeText`) -- the lower box's words join the upper one's on a new line. Title, author and byline boxes aren't merged. On touch screens, swiping a Text view box left removes it (to the archive if it has words; `pbArchiveTextBox`), with Undo in the toast. Every new text box's words are **left-aligned** (never centered by default; Jake's rule), including matter pages; alignment is per box, under Formatting > Alignment. In Spread view a text box or illustration can be dragged onto any other spread (`pbCrossDrag`; the sheet scrolls at the window's edge), and in Text view an illustration's chip drags to another page like a text box's grip. **Cut / Copy / Paste** (Cmd/Ctrl+X, C, V while not typing; `pbClip`) work on a whole selected text box or illustration, pasting on the selected item's spread, else the one last clicked; a spread's ⋯ menu also has Paste while something is cut or copied. **Snap story text to the top or bottom of the page** (`snapText`, on unless turned off; `pbSnapText`): a story box in a page's upper half sits on the top margin, else the bottom one, with its words (what the lines actually take up, not the box) centered on the page; not for a box across the fold, one placed in the page's middle, matter pages, or bound title boxes. Formatting, Front and Back Matter and Export are in every view; outside Text view they start folded. Formatting opens on **Whole book**; a switch at its top (`pbFmtScope`) goes to **This text box** / **This illustration** once one is selected, and back to the book when nothing is. **Front and Back Matter** (`renderPbMatter`) picks a standard layout (`PB_LAYOUTS`, applied by `pbApplyLayout`), sets each front and back page's role, and edits the dedication and copyright wording. Sliders offer about ten steps each (a text box is at most a page wide). On phones (≤ 860px) the tools column sits below the book, so a **Formatting** button at the top of every editor (`.fmt-jump`, in place of Hide tools) opens that panel and scrolls to it. Only the first three and last two pages can take a non-story role (pp. 4–5 and the third-last only when they follow endpapers); `pbRoleOptions` decides what each page may be from the page before it, and `pbApplyRole` lays out that role's usual text.
- **Comments** are in every book type: a text book's margin comments pin to a line; a picture book's to a spread, a cartoon batch's to a cartoon, a comic's to a page, each from that thing's ⋯ menu (**Comment…**), kept in its `comments` and listed under it with Resolve (`commentListFor`, `addCommentTo`). Never printed, and left out of share links.
- **Share a read-only link** (the open book's ⋯, signed in): `?read=<token>` opens the book full screen, page by page, storage kept in memory, no sign-in. The link's token is kept on the book (`shareToken`). Every book type can be shared (`shareTarget`; the reader in `bootReader` opens each type's own full-screen view; pictures come through `?share=<token>&image=<id>`, only ones the shared book uses). The backend keeps each link's `kind` and strips comments, illustration notes and the archive from what readers get (`sharedCopy`).
- **Comics** (`form: 'comic'`; the UI says "comic"; its own library `cmLibrary`, synced as `comicBooks` / `comicBookTrash`, ids `cmk…`, art in the same image store as picture books) are pages of **panels** and **balloons**, edited one page at a time as SVG, with a page strip above (drag a thumbnail to reorder) and the page's ⋯ menu (add, duplicate, move, **Scan drawings into panels…**, clear, archive). Panels are stored in fractions of the page's **gutter frame** (the live area grown by half a gutter all round) and drawn inset by half a gutter (`cmPanelGeom`, `cmOffsetPoly`), so neighbors are always exactly one gutter apart and changing **Gutter**, **Space between tiers**, **Margins** or **Page size** reflows every page. Panels come from the **Grid** stepper (rows × columns), a **template** (`CM_TEMPLATES`) or are drawn with the panel tools (rectangle, oval, slant, triangle; edges snap to the margins and other panels); a panel can be split, bled off the page, and hold one illustration (drop, pick, or scan; click a selected panel with art to **Frame** it: drag and zoom). Balloon styles (`CM_BALLOON_KINDS`): speech, thought, shout, whisper, radio, caption, sound effect (Bangers). Drag a box to draw one (or click for a default size), drag its red dot to aim the tail; it opens a **Words** pop-up and hugs its words (`cmFitBalloon`) unless resized by hand (`sized`). Lettering is book-wide (Formatting: face from `CM_FONTS`, loaded with the editor, bold, all caps, size). The same geometry draws the editor, the page strip, Read full screen and the PDF (`cmDrawPage`), so they match. A panel's art removed by a new layout or Remove goes to the comic's archive, as does a page with art or words; Undo (⌘Z) covers every edit (`cmCheckpoint`). The editor has two **modes** (`cmMode`, remembered per device): **Layout** (panel tools; panels move, resize, split; balloons faded and untouchable) and **Edit** (art, balloons, the **paint bucket**; panels are selected only for their art, never moved). Panels are outlines unless the bucket fills them (`fill`); it also fills balloons, a sound effect's letters, and the page round the panels (`page.bg`). **Layers** (Grid in non-photo blue at a chosen spacing, Balloons, Panel borders, Art and color; `layers`, per comic) can each be hidden, and a hidden layer is left off the PDF too. Scanning uses the cartoon batch's importer (`openCartoonImport` with a `sink`), and **Clean up** reuses its paper-shadow and line-art fixes.
- **Scripts** (`SCRIPT_SPECS`, `scriptElements`, `layoutScript`) are typed in plain text with Fountain's conventions (capitals line over a speech = character, `(parenthetical)`, INT./EXT. heading, `TO:` transition, `>centered<`, `! @ . >` to force) and set on the industry page: US Letter, Courier 12 at 6 lines/inch (54 per page), 1.5 in left margin, page numbers top right from page 2 with a period, a title page (no cover), no folded booklet export. Screenplay: a scene's title is its slugline; scenes run on (the run's first scene holds the pages, the rest point at it with `host`/`hostPage`); speeches break with (MORE)/(CONT'D). Play: each scene starts a page under its act and scene; names centered, directions indented and italic. The editor tags each line (`data-sel`) so it sits where it prints. A script's scene title alignment is fixed by format (screenplay left, play centered; `titleAlignFor`). In a play, Enter on an empty line steps it along (`playEnter`): under a speech, a stage direction; then a character name; then the likeliest speaker filled in (`likelyNextSpeaker`). A play has a **character list** in Front and Back Matter (`castList`, `castPagePlan`; `includeCast`, `castHeading`, `castNotes`, `castHidden`), a page after the title page. With Front and Back Matter open on a page left out of the book, the preview still shows that page, marked as not included.
- **Importing many files at once** (`importFileBatch`): a Drive folder or several picked files (`openDriveFilesPicker`, multiselect Picker; a folder's contents are listed through the Drive API, and if `drive.file` hides them the person is told to open the folder and select the files), or PDFs/text from the computer. Files go in file-name order (numeric-aware), each parsed on its own; PDFs are read with pdf.js from cdnjs, loaded on first use (`pdfToDocText`: lines rebuilt from positions, bigger gaps = stanza breaks, larger type = titles, lone page numbers dropped). A file with no title of its own takes a short first line standing alone, else its file name minus any leading number (`withFileTitle`, `fileTitle`).

- **Tiers** (poetry collections only): a poem can be marked with one of the person's own named tiers (default Finished, Needs revision, Draft) from the pill beside its ⋯. - **Series** (poetry collections; a poem's ⋯ › Make a series): one poem in **parts** (never "sections", which divide the book), each starting a new page. The parts are plain marker lines in the poem. A bar above a series in Poem View (`makeSeriesBar`) picks its numbering, `poem.seriesStyle` (`{ kind, period, prefix }`: Roman, numbers, words, letters, Hebrew, Greek, or a section-break symbol from `SERIES_SYMBOLS`), and has **+ Add part**, which adds a part after the one the cursor is in and renumbers the rest (`renumberedLines`); parts typed in by hand are renumbered when the cursor leaves the poem. A series with no `seriesStyle` keeps the old rule: markers counting 1, 2, 3 in order. **Titles & order** in the same bar lists the parts: each can have a title (`poem.partTitles`, one per part in order, printed in italics on the line under its marker and shown there in Poem View) and moves up or down whole, with its title, then the numbers are put back in order. Titles follow the part's position, so + Add part and moving parts keep them in step; a part typed or deleted by hand doesn't.
- A text book entry's ⋯ menu groups its moving and copying into two submenus (`openEntrySub`): **Move ›** (Move to top, top/bottom of section, bottom, Move to another section…, Move to another book…) and **Copy ›** (Duplicate, Copy to another section…, Copy to another book…).
